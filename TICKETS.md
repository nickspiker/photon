## Fleet-assisted attest — "I hold the vault, my friend holds the key" (designed 2026-08-07; build once messages flow)

Auto-attest after reboot with NO durable identity secret at rest. The capsule stores identity_seed, whose handle derivation is deliberately CHEAP (`blake3(x(handle))` — handle_proof is the memory-hard one), so a stolen durable capsule falls to a dictionary sweep of human handles and then passes even the lock-out's handle gate: never seal it under a machine fact. Instead:
- Waking device stores OUTSIDE the vault: `wake.manifest` (handle_proof + sibling device pubkeys — public, addressing only) and `wake.capsule` (the 96-byte SessionIdentity sealed under a random `wake_key` the device does NOT keep).
- Each sibling holds `wake_key` in its own vault under the fleet key, keyed by the waking device's pubkey.
- Enable (offered only with ≥1 sibling; Android additionally only when `KeyguardManager.isDeviceSecure() == false` — on a locked device FBE blocks pre-unlock reads anyway and direct-boot storage is the weak store, so capability check = risk check): mint key, seal capsule, push shares sealed to each sibling's device key, require ≥1 ACK, zeroize.
- Boot: derive device keypair from the machine fact (survives reboot), read manifest, open the relay pipe (keyed by device pubkey — works vault-less), send signed WAKE_REQUEST to siblings over relay + LAN.
- Sibling grants iff: signature valid AND requester in current fold AND NOT in the locked set — then seals wake_key to the requester's device pubkey (asymmetric; requester has no vault yet), logs it, raises a fleet-inbox notice ("DeviceX woke at T").
- On grant: open capsule, attest, immediately ROTATE (fresh wake_key, re-seal, re-distribute) — a captured share is single-use.
- No sibling reachable → wait and retry forever, alive-but-unattested; manual handle attest always works. One-device fleets: physically impossible to enable (no share can exist) — the UI only reports it.
- Policy knob: auto-grant (default, the unattended property) vs require-approval (closes the "thief powers it on before you lock it out" window at the cost of unattendedness — user's call). Residual risk in the warning either way.
- Later: TPM/StrongBox/SE wrap of the LOCAL half for the headless-Linux node (the one genuinely good TPM use here); fleet veto stays on top. Related: docs/device-trust-and-recovery.md, the 2026-08-05/06 lockout stack.
