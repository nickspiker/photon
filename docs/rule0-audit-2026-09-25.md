# Rule 0 audit — 2026-09-25

AGENT.md Rule 0: every bounds check, saturating/checked/wrapping op, clamp or constraining min/max must **state why, prove it was necessary, and explain what it prevents** — and one the math already rules out is defensive noise that hides the bug it pretends to guard. The Clamp Trap adds that a cast already bounds what it casts.

**The fact that set the stakes.** Both `dev` and `release` build with `overflow-checks = false` and `debug-assertions = false`. Plain arithmetic that CAN overflow wraps silently in every build, and `debug_assert!` never fires. So a guard was deleted only where the invariant truly holds, and "fail loud" means `assert!`/`expect`, never `debug_assert!`.

The proof convention is a `// WHY:` line and a `// PROOF:` line, or `// WHY/PROOF:` when one sentence carries both; an algorithmic bound says `the algorithm:` instead, because it is the math rather than a guard.

### The rules that decided most sites

- **Differences of our own eagle stamps cannot overflow** (both positive, < 9.2e18) — deleted. **A peer's stamp can be anything** — kept: an absurd stamp must read as stale, not wrap `now − i64::MIN` into a fresh-looking negative.
- **u32 counters bumped once per event never reach 2^32** — deleted. **u8/u16 counters really do pass their ceiling**, and a wrap re-fires or hides the edge they gate — kept.
- **A float → int `as` cast saturates** (and sends NaN to 0), so a clamp or `.max(0.0)` before it computes nothing — deleted (84 of them across tiers 2 and 4c). **An int → int cast wraps**, so there the clamp is the saturating mix — kept. **A u32 packed into a pixel** saturates at 2^32 not 255, so a colour clip before it is load-bearing — kept.
- **Repeated idioms became one helper with one proof** instead of a hundred copies: `mean_abs`/`mean_abs_24` (a frame's level), `hairline_px` (a stroke is at least one device pixel), `disk_px` (an avatar is at least one pixel across), `frac_of` (nothing of nothing is 0, not NaN), `is_fresh` (a peer record's age), `reserve_hits` (hit-id spans, fail-loud like fluor's `next_id`).

## Bugs the guards were hiding (fixed)

- **`fmt_share` of a whole** scaled to 12^5, whose first digit is twelve; `.min(11)` rendered it as eleven twelfths — the volume rocker at full read `.Ɛ`, hex read `0.FFF`. A whole now reads as the whole one; part ≤ whole is asserted and external values are bounded where they enter.
- **`encode_vsf_length(0)`** silently wrote a length of ONE (the saturating `len − 1`). It now fails loud.
- **Hit ids** were handed out with `wrapping_add`, so a leaking allocation pattern would have wrapped into `HIT_NONE` and aliased controls silently. Spans now come from a fail-loud allocator.
- **`parse_dozenal_bytes`** — a text-number parser for the retired marker grammar, no caller left — deleted.

## Real problems found, NOT fixed here (each wants its own change)

1. **FIXED (2026-09-25): A stale contact index can name a different contact.** ~80 `self.contacts.get(ci)` sites carry an index across a deferral (a queued job, a deferred send, a hit id, a worker's reply). `.get` stops the panic when the vec shrinks, but after a removal the same index can point at the NEXT contact. Stable contact ids instead of indices are the fix. Done: everything that outlives the call that found its contact — the deferred send queue, the bridge executor's emits and pigeon landings, the attachment fetch retries, the env wants, the open viewer, the strip selection/dismissal/deferred delete, the orb, the presence probe and the wrap cache — now carries the 32-byte `ContactId` and resolves it with `ci_of` when it runs; a contact removed in between drops the job instead of re-aiming it. Indices collected and applied inside one call stay indices (nothing in those calls shrinks the list). Group rows (`molecule_rosters` indices) are the same shape and not yet converted.
2. **FIXED (2026-09-25): Peer freshness has no upper bound.** A peer record whose signed `last_seen` is in the FUTURE never expires in the peer store and wins every gossip merge; the hist_req and blind-frame staleness checks likewise accept any future stamp. A freshness window needs a ceiling (now + the tolerated clock skew).
3. **FIXED (2026-09-25): Attachment progress is keyed by peer address, not by blob.** `attach_progress` counts every outbound attachment in flight, so two sends at once miscount each other's bars (the saturating subtraction now says so).
4. **FIXED (2026-09-25): `Textbox.cursor` and `.chars` are public** and photon rewrites `chars` directly (the rename prefill) without moving the cursor, so the type cannot hold cursor ≤ len and every reader must clamp. A setter in fluor that keeps the invariant is the fix. Done: `set_text` on Textbox and MultiTextbox (fluor), and photon's four direct writers (the device rename, the molecule title, the new-atom clear, a no-op clear) go through it or `clear()`.
5. **Parallel-column decoders** (molecule records, chain pendings, era KEMs, history pages) zip columns that a malformed record can carry at different lengths. The zips are proven, but AGENT.md prefers nested sections to parallel arrays — the same shape the format audit flagged.

~55 maps. None carries consent today. Recommendation per map:

**Keep — N scales with rows, windows or blobs, and the map is hit inside a loop** (the proof is the O(1)-vs-O(N) lookup at that N): the history-merge `existing_idx` (conversation rows × page rows), the recording's `fill_frames` (thousands of windows), render's `edit_over` and `rec_over` (per visible row), `img_cache`, `wave_env`, `wave_fold_cache`, `avatar_probe_cache`, `egged_cache` (per visible row per frame), `attach_chunk_progress`, `attach_fetch_inflight`, `attach_served_recent`, `pigeon_progress`, pigeon `inflight`.

## Not covered by this pass

About 300 `min`/`max` calls that are not in a guard shape — layout arithmetic ("the larger of these two widths"), maxima over data, rate limits — were not individually proven. They constrain nothing in Rule 0's sense; a later pass could still find a disguised clamp among them.
